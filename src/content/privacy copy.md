# 1. Introduction

Iursperitis ("the Application") is an Anti-Money Laundering, Counter-Financing of Terrorism, and Counter-Proliferation Financing (AML/CFT/CPF) compliance management system provided by Iuris Peritis to enable advocates, legal practitioners, and law firms in Tanzania to discharge their obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering Act, Cap. 423, the Proceeds of Crime Act, Cap. 256, the Prevention of Terrorism Act, Cap. 19, and related regulations and guidelines issued by the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) and the Tanganyika Law Society (TLS).

The Application supports law firms in conducting Know-Your-Client (KYC) due diligence on their clients, screening clients against international sanctions lists (United Nations, OFAC SDN, UK HMT/OFSI, EU consolidated list), identifying politically exposed persons (PEPs), assessing client risk, monitoring matters for AML/CFT/CPF red flags, maintaining the records required by law, and preparing the information needed to file Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) or Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) with the FIU where required.

This Policy explains the personal data processed through the Application, the reasons for such processing, how we safeguard such data, and the rights you hold as a data subject under the Personal Data Protection Act, Cap. 44 (Act No. 11 of 2022) ("the PDP Act"). It is to be read alongside our Terms and Conditions of Use and any sector-specific guidance issued by the FIU and the TLS.

## 1.1 Status of Iuris Peritis Under the PDP Act

Iuris Peritis is officially registered with the Personal Data Protection Commission in accordance with section 14 of the PDP Act, as a Data Processor, under Certificate No. 0-000-006-655, issued on 31 March 2026. This status is described in detail in Section 6 of this Policy.

In short, section 3 of the PDP Act defines a Data Processor as a natural person, legal person or public body which processes personal data for and on behalf of the Data Controller and under the Data Controller's instruction. In the context of Iursperitis:

- **The User (advocate or law firm) is the Data Controller** of all client data they enter into the Application — KYC profiles of their clients, identification documents, transactional information, source-of-funds declarations, risk assessments, sanctions screening results, and matter records. The User decides which clients to onboard, what data to collect, for what purposes, and for what duration, in accordance with their AML/CFT/CPF obligations under Tanzanian law.

- **Iuris Peritis is the Data Processor** that provides the Application and the underlying technical infrastructure. The Application processes data automatically based on the User's instructions (through their use of the Application's features — onboarding a client, running a sanctions screen, recording a risk assessment, generating a compliance report) and produces the outputs required by the User to meet their regulatory obligations.

Iuris Peritis does not read, analyse, or use the substantive content of client KYC or matter data entered by Users for its own commercial purposes. We do not sell such data, and we do not share it with third parties except in the limited circumstances described in Section 11 of this Policy (including, where required by law, lawful access by the FIU and other competent authorities).

# 4. Data Processed Through the Application

The following data is processed by the Application on behalf of the User. The User is the Data Controller, and Iuris Peritis is the Data Processor that ensures this data is stored securely and processed automatically in line with the Application's features.

When a law firm opens an account, the following data relating to the firm and its authorised users is entered and stored:

- Authorised user details: full name, professional position (Principal Advocate, Associate, Compliance Officer, Paralegal, Support Staff), email address, mobile number.

- Authentication data: passwords (stored only as a secure hash, never in plain text), JWT session tokens, Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) secrets and backup codes.

- Subscription data: chosen tier (Small Firm, Medium Firm, Large Firm), billing cycle, payment reference numbers from mobile money services (Mixx by Yas, M-Pesa, Airtel Money, HaloPesa) processed via ClickPesa, or bank transfer references for CRDB Bank deposits. We do not store mobile money PINs, wallet secrets, or full bank account credentials.

## 4.2 Internal User Records

When the User adds team members to the Application (associates, paralegals, compliance officers, support staff), the following data is processed:

- Name, assigned role within the Application (Management, Lawyer, Compliance Officer, Staff, Client view), secure hash of authentication credentials, sign-in records, and audit-log entries reflecting actions taken in the Application.

The User, as employer, has a duty to inform their team members that the Application is in use and that it records their professional activity for compliance and audit purposes. The User is responsible for obtaining any consent required from team members under the PDP Act. Iuris Peritis, as Data Processor, only stores this data on behalf of the User.

## 4.3 Client KYC and Due Diligence Data

This is the central category of data processed by the Application. When the User onboards a client and performs customer due diligence in accordance with the Anti-Money Laundering Act, the following data is processed:

- Client identification: full legal name, date of birth, nationality, gender, occupation, employer, residential and postal address, telephone, email.

- Identification documents: national identification number, passport number, driver's licence, voter card, taxpayer identification number (TIN), and uploaded copies of such documents where the User chooses to attach them to the client profile.

- Beneficial ownership information for non-individual clients (companies, trusts, partnerships, NGOs): names of directors, shareholders, settlors, trustees, beneficiaries, and ultimate beneficial owners, along with the identification details set out above.

- Source-of-funds and source-of-wealth declarations provided by the client or recorded by the advocate based on the client's instructions.

- Politically Exposed Person (PEP) status and screening results, including matches found against PEP databases and the User's determination of whether the client (or their beneficial owner) is a PEP.

- Sanctions screening results, including matches found against the United Nations consolidated sanctions list, the OFAC SDN list maintained by the United States Office of Foreign Assets Control, the UK consolidated sanctions list maintained by the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI), the EU consolidated list, and any other sanctions or watchlist data sources made available by the Application.

- Risk classification (Low, Medium, High) assigned by the User after performing the risk-based assessment required by the AML/CFT/CPF framework, together with the supporting rationale entered by the User.

- Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) records where the client is rated High Risk or is a PEP, including the additional approvals, controls, and documentation required by the Anti-Money Laundering Regulations.

## 4.4 Matter Data

When a matter is opened in the Application for a client, the following data is processed:

- Matter title, description, area of law, retainer or fee arrangement, value of the transaction or estate concerned where applicable, dates of instruction and disengagement, counterparties to the transaction, and a narrative of the activities undertaken by the advocate sufficient for AML/CFT/CPF audit purposes.

- Ongoing-monitoring observations: notes captured by the User during the life of the matter recording unusual activity, transaction patterns, or other red flags, together with the User's analysis.

## 4.5 STR/SAR Preparatory Data

Where the User determines, in accordance with the Anti-Money Laundering Act, that a transaction or attempted transaction is suspicious, the Application enables the User to record and prepare the information needed to file a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) or Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) with the FIU. The Application stores:

- The trigger event or red flag observed by the User, the date and time of the observation, the affected client and matter, the User's narrative analysis, the reporting officer's name, and the date of any report ultimately filed with the FIU through the FIU's own reporting channel.

STR/SAR-related data is treated by the Application as the most sensitive class of data. It is subject to additional access controls (see Section 8.8), is excluded from data subject rights to the extent required by section 33(2) of the PDP Act (which permits a Data Controller not to inform a data subject where the data is involved in an investigation in accordance with the laws) and the tipping-off prohibition under the Anti-Money Laundering Act, and is retained for ten (10) years from the date of the underlying transaction.

## 4.6 Technical and Operational Data

This data is collected automatically by the Application for security and service-operation purposes — for this category Iuris Peritis determines the purpose of processing, as it relates to the integrity of the platform rather than to substantive compliance activity:

- Device type, operating system, application version, browser fingerprint, error logs, event logs.

- IP addresses recorded in security audit logs for login-attempt tracking, account lockout, geographic anomaly detection, and rate-limiting purposes.

- Payment webhook metadata: transaction identifiers, signature verification outcomes, source IP addresses of incoming webhooks, processing status, and timestamps, used to maintain a tamper-evident payment audit trail.

Iuris Peritis acts as Data Processor for all client KYC, matter, and STR-related data entered by the User. # 5. Legal Basis for Processing

As a Data Processor, Iuris Peritis relies on the following lawful bases, all of which are permitted under the PDP Act:

- **Instructions of the Data Controller** — the primary basis for a Data Processor. We process client KYC, matter, and STR-related data on behalf of the User who has instructed us to do so by accepting our Terms of Use and using the features of the Application.

- **Use authorised or required by law** — under section 25(2)(b) and section 25(2)(f) of the PDP Act, personal data may be used for a purpose authorised or required by law and where necessary for compliance with the laws. The Anti-Money Laundering Act requires Reporting Persons (including advocates carrying out specified activities) to keep KYC and transaction records for not less than ten (10) years. The Application facilitates the User's compliance with this statutory retention obligation.

- **Performance of a function of the Data Controller** — under section 22(2) of the PDP Act, personal data may be collected for a lawful purpose related to a function of the Data Controller. The User's professional functions as an advocate include compliance with AML/CFT/CPF legal obligations and representation of clients.

- **Contract performance** — the service contract between the User (the law firm) and Iuris Peritis. This applies particularly to account data, subscription payments, and the authentication required to provide the service.

## 5.1 The User's Obligations as Data Controller

Because the User is the Data Controller of all client KYC and matter data entered into the Application, the User is responsible for ensuring that they:

- Have a lawful basis for collecting such data — most commonly, the legal obligation imposed by the Anti-Money Laundering Act to identify and verify the identity of clients, combined with the advocate-client relationship and the necessity of representing the client.

- Have given appropriate notice to clients about how their data will be processed for AML/CFT/CPF purposes and for the conduct of the matter, in accordance with section 23(2) of the PDP Act.

- Are registered with the Personal Data Protection Commission as a Data Controller where their professional activities require it under section 14 of the PDP Act. - Honour data subject rights requests directed to them, with the carve-outs allowed by section 33(2) of the PDP Act (data involved in an investigation) and by the Anti-Money Laundering Act (in particular the tipping-off prohibition, which restricts disclosure to data subjects of any STR-related information).

Iuris Peritis assists the User in meeting these obligations by providing tools that enable the User to access, rectify, export, or — at the end of the statutory retention period — securely delete the data processed through the Application.

## 5.2 Sensitive Personal Data

Section 30(1) of the PDP Act prohibits the processing of sensitive personal data without the prior written consent of the data subject, unless one of the exceptions in section 30(5) applies. The Application is designed to process personal data for AML/CFT/CPF purposes and may, in the ordinary course of customer due diligence, capture data that falls within the definition of sensitive personal data in section 3 of the Act. # 7. Rights of Data Subjects

Part VI of the PDP Act (sections 33 to 38) sets out the rights of data subjects. The User of the Application, as Data Controller, is responsible for honouring these rights in respect of the data the User holds about its clients, employees, and counterparties. Iuris Peritis, as Data Processor, provides tools and reasonable assistance to enable the User to discharge these obligations.

Because Iuris Peritis is a Data Processor, rights requests in respect of client data should ordinarily be directed to the Data Controller — that is, the law firm that holds the data in its account on the Application. A client (data subject) who wishes to exercise their rights in respect of data held about them by a law firm should contact that firm in the first instance. You may nevertheless contact Iuris Peritis directly, and we will assist you in reaching the relevant Data Controller.

Certain rights are restricted in respect of STR/SAR-related data and other AML/CFT/CPF records. In particular, section 33(2)(b) of the PDP Act permits a Data Controller not to inform a data subject where the personal data is involved in an investigation in accordance with the laws, and the tipping-off prohibition under the Anti-Money Laundering Act restricts disclosure to data subjects of any STR-related information. Where a right is restricted, this Policy and the User's response to the request will say so clearly, without disclosing the existence or content of any report filed with the FIU.

### 7.1 Right of Access to Personal Data (PDP Act, s.33)

Subject to the provisions of the PDP Act, you have the right to be informed by any Data Controller whether your personal data is being processed by or on behalf of that Data Controller, to be given a description of the data, the purposes of processing, and the recipients or classes of recipients to whom the data may be disclosed. Where automated processing forms the sole basis of a decision significantly affecting you, you have the right to be informed of the logic involved in that decision making. The Data Controller is not obliged to inform you where the personal data is not accurate, is involved in an investigation in accordance with the laws, or where disclosure has been prohibited by court order (PDP Act, s.33(2)).

### 7.2 Right to Prevent Processing Likely to Cause Damage (PDP Act, s.34)

You are entitled, through the procedures prescribed in the regulations, to require a Data Controller to suspend or not to begin processing of any personal data in respect of which you are the data subject, if the processing of such personal data is likely to cause substantial damage to you or to another person. This right does not apply in the exceptions provided under the PDP Act, including where the processing is required by law (such as ongoing AML/CFT/CPF retention).

### 7.3 Right to Prevent Processing for Direct Marketing (PDP Act, s.35)

You may require the Data Controller to stop processing your personal data for the purposes of direct marketing. The Application is not used to conduct direct marketing to clients of Users.

### 7.4 Rights in Relation to Automated Decision Making (PDP Act, s.36)

You may require the Data Controller to ensure that any decision taken by or on behalf of the Data Controller which significantly affects you is not based solely on automated processing. Where such a decision is taken solely on the basis of automated processing, the Data Controller is required to notify you and you may require the Data Controller to reconsider the decision. This right does not apply where the decision is necessary for entering into or performance of a contract between you and the Data Controller, is authorised by any written law, or is based on your explicit consent.

The Application supports the User in performing AML/CFT/CPF risk assessments and sanctions screening. The User retains final professional judgment in classifying clients and in deciding whether to enter into or continue a business relationship; decisions affecting the data subject are not made solely on the basis of automated processing within the Application.

### 7.5 Right to Compensation (PDP Act, s.37)

If you suffer damage by reason of any contravention of the requirements of the PDP Act by a Data Controller or Data Processor, you are entitled to compensation from that Data Controller or Data Processor for that damage. "Damage" for this purpose includes financial loss and damage not involving financial loss (PDP Act, s.50(4)).

### 7.6 Rectification, Blocking, Erasure or Destruction of Personal Data (PDP Act, s.38)

Where the Commission is satisfied, on the application of a data subject, that personal data is inaccurate, the Commission may order the Data Controller or Data Processor to rectify, block, erase, or destroy the personal data. Where data has been so rectified, blocked, erased or destroyed, the Data Controller or Data Processor is required to notify third parties to whom the personal data has been previously disclosed of the rectification, blocking, updating, erasure or destruction.

In addition to this statutory route via the Commission, Iuris Peritis provides Users with in-application tools to correct or update data they have entered, in alignment with the data accuracy principle in section 5(d) of the PDP Act and the correction provisions of section 29 of the Act. Erasure of data subject to the ten-year retention obligation under the Anti-Money Laundering Act is not available until the statutory retention period has expired.

# 8. Security of Processing

Iuris Peritis implements appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect personal data, as required by section 27 of the PDP Act. The principal security controls are organised into the following groups.

## 8.1 Authentication and Access Control

- Email/password authentication powered by Supabase Auth, with JSON Web Token (JWT) session tokens.

- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) via Time-based One-Time Password (TOTP), compatible with Google Authenticator, Authy, and 1Password, with single-use backup codes for account recovery. MFA is available for all users; mandatory enforcement for privileged actions is being phased in.

- Strong password requirements: minimum length and complexity enforced server-side; common passwords and sequential patterns rejected.

- Account lockout protection: accounts are locked after a configurable number of failed login attempts per email or per IP address within a sliding time window.

- Global sign-out: Users can revoke all active sessions across devices from a single control.

## 8.2 Data Protection and Encryption

- HTTPS enforcement on all data in transit, with HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) enabled for one (1) year, including subdomains and preload.

- Database encryption at rest, managed by the Supabase infrastructure.

- Row Level Security (RLS) on all database tables, ensuring that each User's queries are restricted at the database layer to the organisation they belong to. Cross-organisation access is structurally impossible at the database layer.

- Ownership-based access: every data record (clients, matters, screenings, STR records) is tied to an organisation; access requires matching organisation identity at the data layer in addition to the role check at the application layer.

## 8.3 Input Sanitisation and Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) Prevention

- HTML sanitisation of all User-supplied free-text fields, with a strict allowlist of safe tags and attributes.

- Script injection blocking: <script>, <iframe>, <object>, <embed>, and <form> elements are stripped from User-supplied content.

- Event handler removal: dangerous attributes (such as onclick, onerror, onload) are blocked.

- Protocol validation: only HTTPS, mailto, and relative URLs are permitted in User-supplied links.

## 8.4 Security Headers

- X-Frame-Options: DENY — prevents clickjacking by blocking iframe embedding.

- X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff — prevents MIME-type sniffing attacks.

- Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin — limits referrer data leakage.

- Permissions-Policy: restrictive settings blocking microphone, geolocation, and other sensitive browser APIs by default.

- Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy and Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: configured to prevent cross-origin information leakage.

- Content Security Policy (CSP): restricts script execution and external content loading.

## 8.5 Rate Limiting and Abuse Prevention

- Login rate limiting: per-email and per-IP thresholds enforced within sliding time windows.

- Sanctions screening rate limiting: plan-based quotas to prevent abuse of the screening feature.

- Payment webhook idempotency: duplicate payment processing is prevented by a database-level unique constraint on the combination of provider transaction identifier and status, complemented by application-level idempotency checks against the organisation's subscription state.

## 8.6 Secure API Key Management

- Server-side key isolation: all third-party API keys (ClickPesa, Resend, Supabase service role) are stored as server-side environment variables and are never exposed to client code.

- Edge function proxying: all external API calls (payment initiation, status polling, email dispatch) are routed through secure server-side Edge Functions that validate the User's authentication and role before forwarding requests.

- Webhook signature verification: payment webhooks are verified using HMAC-SHA256 with timing-safe comparison, a sixty-minute timestamp validation window, and a complete audit log of every webhook delivery attempt, whether accepted or rejected.

## 8.7 Session Management

- JWT access tokens with short expiry (approximately one (1) hour) and automatic refresh through Supabase Auth.

- Server-side refresh token revocation on sign-out, ensuring revoked tokens cannot be reused.

- Session cleanup: stale and orphaned sessions are automatically purged.

- Single-device sign-out and global sign-out: global revocation terminates all active sessions across all devices.

## 8.8 Data Access Controls

- Role-based access control with the following roles: System Admin, Management, Lawyer, Compliance Officer, Staff, Client view. Each role's permissions are defined and enforced at both the application and the database layer.

- STR/SAR records are visible only to the Management and Compliance Officer roles, and the existence of any STR is excluded from any client-facing view of the client's profile, to honour the tipping-off prohibition under the Anti-Money Laundering Act.

- Plan-based feature gating: different subscription tiers have enforced limits on users, clients, and screenings, applied through subscription-state checks before privileged operations.

- Database triggers and Row Level Security policies: server-side enforcement of organisation isolation and role boundaries, applied even if client-side checks are bypassed (consistent with the disclosure limitation in section 26 of the PDP Act).

## 8.9 Audit Logging

- Security event logging: password changes, MFA enrolment/reset, role changes, and global sign-outs are recorded with timestamp, IP address, and user agent.

- Compliance event logging: client onboarding, KYC field changes, sanctions screening runs, risk classification changes, and STR-related activity are recorded in a tamper-evident audit trail with timestamps, the responsible user, and the previous and new values.

- Webhook audit trail: every incoming payment webhook is logged with signature verification result, processing outcome, source IP address, and correlation identifiers — whether the webhook was accepted, rejected for stale timestamp, or rejected for invalid signature.

- Account lockout logging: lockout events are recorded with severity classification for security review. The Commission's investigation powers under section 42 of the PDP Act are supported by the completeness of these logs.

## 8.10 Payment Security

Subscription payments are processed exclusively through audited channels. The principal controls are:

- Mobile money payments (Mixx by Yas, M-Pesa, Airtel Money, HaloPesa) are processed via ClickPesa Limited, with the customer entering the payment PIN directly with their mobile network operator. No PIN or wallet secret is ever seen by the Application or by ClickPesa.

- Bank transfer payments to CRDB Bank are settled directly between the customer and the bank; the Application only records the reference number and reconciles the deposit against the customer's claim by a member of the firm's management.

- Webhook signature verification uses HMAC-SHA256 with timing-safe comparison.

- Timestamp validation: webhooks older than sixty (60) minutes are rejected and the rejection is logged for audit purposes.

- Duplicate prevention: a database-level unique constraint on (transaction identifier, status) prevents double-processing of the same payment event; the subscription-activation routine is idempotent and safe to call multiple times for the same payment.

- Backup polling: an independent scheduled task verifies the status of any payment that remains in a "processing" state for more than five minutes, providing redundancy against webhook delivery failures.
